Use 3D printing for brackets, mounts, spacers, small mechanical details, and parts that need quick iteration.

## Materials

We use [PETG-HF](https://eu.store.bambulab.com/de/products/petg-hf) in the paperlesspaper hardware because it is durable, has good layer adhesion, and prints well without warping. It also has a nice matte finish that hides layer lines.

PLA is also a good choice since it is usually hidden inside the frame and does not require high heat resistance. It is easier to print and in our case usually not affected by the heat and UV exposure.

![3D printed parts](./IMG_5980-Bearbeitet.jpg)

## Baseline slicer settings

Start with these settings unless the model notes say otherwise:

- 0.2 mm layer height
- 3 or more perimeters
- 20-30% infill
- supports only where the model requires them
- print orientation that keeps visible faces clean and mechanical layers strong

![3D printed parts](./IMG_2525.jpeg)

### Parts

- `Upper spacing` right
- `USB-part` from the right
- `Battery compartment` bottom left
- `Battery cover` (consists of flat battery cover and battery snap lock) top left

### Glue

The battery cover must be glued together. It is best to wear one glove so that no glue gets on your hand and to place cardboard underneath. Use only waterproof cyanoacrylate super glue (do not use spray adhesive)!

<Callout type="tip" title="Glue application">
  Make sure that no glue leaks out at the sides, but apply enough glue so that
  the bond is sufficiently strong.
</Callout>

![Lots of parts](./IMG_7602.jpeg)

#### Procedure

- Lay out about 10–20 covers.
- Apply a small amount of super glue to the cover.
- Glue in the latch.
- Press briefly (possibly press firmly again after about 2 minutes).
- Leave to rest (about 5 minutes).
- Check whether the glued connection holds sufficiently.
