## Partial updates and the area outside the window

For a field that must erase as well as draw, write the replacement pixels into a window and let the waveform finish. At the fast clock, a 240 × 400 window covering **5% of the panel still took about 15 seconds**, about as long as the whole screen. Sending `0x01` with the refresh command (`0x12`) kept the surrounding dark, red and blue patches close to their original appearance during repeated black/white updates. The active window still flashed.

## Full-frame speed, flashing and color

For a photo frame, compare the settled blue and green before choosing a clock. For a frequently replaced dashboard, the fastest tested clock saved about 21 seconds per full draw. All three settings flash. Register `0x30` selects the clock. BUSY time measures the panel drive after image transfer.

| Clock setting | Register value | BUSY in this comparison | Settled color |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Normal (dynamic) | `0x08` | 35.8 s | Reference |
| Nominal 75 Hz | `0x04` | 23.9 s | Green shifts toward yellow |
| Nominal 120 Hz | `0x07` | 14.9 s | Blue becomes paler and shows a vertical band |

## Color photos with less grain

The `raw16` photo profile lets Floyd–Steinberg dithering choose among all 16 four-bit image codes in one normal refresh. On frame-2 those codes produced **seven distinguishable groups of solid colors**, including a muted blue. Across 12 uniform patches, measured grain fell **22%** compared with six-code dithering. Average color error across the three photographs stayed about the same.

To encode your own photograph, unpack the [color photo renderer (ZIP)](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/2026-11-21/photo-renderer.zip), install NumPy and Pillow, and run these commands in the extracted directory. The two output folders contain the image RAM for each controller half on the bench. The editable [color LUT (JSON)](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/2026-11-21/palette.json) pairs measured colors with pixel codes. The controller supplies the voltage waveform. [Paired measurements and two-pass photo trials](/panels/dke-13-3-inch#color-photo-dithering-measurements).

```sh
cc -O3 -fPIC -shared diffuse.c -o diffuse.so -lm
python3 render_photo.py photo.jpg six-code-output --mode normal
python3 render_photo.py photo.jpg sixteen-code-output --mode raw16
```

## Native colors, orange and skin tones

The six standard image codes produce black, white, red, yellow, blue and green. Mixing adjacent red and yellow pixels gave repeatable saturated orange. For pale pink, a 4 × 4 tile used 14 white pixels and two red ones. A separate two-pass test produced a **solid peach** suitable for light skin. It prepared the pixel with red (`0x3`) and drove blue (`0x5`) for 2.8 s. [Solid-color measurements](/panels/dke-13-3-inch#two-pass-solid-colors).

## Setting a custom LUT

**You can edit the grayscale photo LUT in the bench firmware.** It maps image brightness to two-pass pixel recipes. For example, `FD` prepares a pixel with code `F` and drives it with `D` during the timed update. Loading custom voltage waveforms into the NT61522 remains unverified. [Controller references](/panels/dke-13-3-inch#controller-waveform-references).

1. Save the [example photo LUT (CSV)](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/control/photo-lut.csv?build=6382664) and [Python encoder/decoder](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/control/patch_palette.py) in the same directory.
2. Edit the two-hex-digit `recipe` and its inclusive brightness limit, `upper_luma` (0–255). Use 16 rows with nondecreasing thresholds ending at 255. Repeat 255 to fill unused rows.
3. Generate the command in that directory.

```sh
python3 patch_palette.py photo photo-lut.csv
```

Send the printed `FINEPHOTO` line followed by a newline to the bench serial console at 115200 baud. It renders the built-in Apollo photo with your table and a 2.8 s partial pulse. [Photo rendered with the example LUT](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/control/apollo-host-lut.webp?build=6382664).
