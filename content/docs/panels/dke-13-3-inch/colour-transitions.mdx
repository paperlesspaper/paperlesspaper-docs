## Reading the camera measurements

- **Drive** runs from the refresh command to the controller’s BUSY-ready edge. **First visible change** is the first of two successive camera samples with at least one RGB channel more than eight levels from its starting value. Each channel ranges from 0 to 255.
- **Optically stable by** is the first sample after the last deviation of more than eight levels in any channel from the final colour. At least 0.35 seconds of observation must remain. A patch can settle at the wrong colour. “Unresolved” means the recording did not establish the timing.
- **Dark / bright flashes** count separate excursions more than 20 camera brightness levels below both endpoints or above both endpoints, respectively. For another flash of the same kind to count, the brightness must first return to within 10 levels of the endpoint range or cross back into it. Expand **Flash timing** to see the first and last camera sample of each excursion, measured from the refresh command. **Brightness reversals** also catch visible changes of direction *between* the endpoints. A peak or trough counts after a counter-movement of at least 20 levels. Two reversals can belong to the same flash. These optical counts describe the camera patch. Electrical pulses can be faster than the camera.
- The **colour strip** follows the camera's RGB readings. The line above it shows brightness, with dots at detected reversals and a dashed line at the end of electrical drive. Optical measurements use three-frame median smoothing. Timing is always in real seconds, including when the native clip is playing at 4× speed.

We scored 77 pulse and timing configurations per direction. A match requires both halves to finish closest to the requested colour with an RMS distance of 0.10 or less. We ranked matches ahead of approximations and misses. For an approximation or miss, we considered endpoints within 0.13 of the best distance in that class. We then preferred fewer brightness reversals, fewer dark or bright excursions beyond the endpoints, and a shorter drive, in that order. This favours a steadier patch when both colours receive the same grade. Both the previous and new clips remain available. The matrix opens the better of the two by these criteria.

### Can the panel clock run faster?

Register `0x30` selects the panel clock. In an earlier full-colour comparison, a complete drive at the normal dynamic setting (`0x08`) took 35.8 s, the nominal 75 Hz setting (`0x04`) took 23.9 s and the nominal 120 Hz setting (`0x07`) took 14.9 s. At 120 Hz, blue was paler and showed a vertical band. All the partial-drive trials here already use `0x07`. [Watch the three clocks side by side](/panels/dke-13-3-inch/display-control#full-frame-speed-flashing-and-color).

We also changed the frequency index in the waveform table while holding the PLL at `0x07`. Each run used the same seven 96-period source sequences in the same positions. The controller reported the following times from the refresh command to BUSY-ready.

| Waveform index | Programmed periods | Measured drive |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | 96 | 808.2 ms |
| 6 | 96 | 808.1 ms |
| 4 | 96 | 808.0 ms |

The three BUSY times differed by less than 0.2 ms across the entire drive. Changing this waveform field produced essentially the same duration at the fixed 120 Hz PLL setting. A waveform period is one stage of pigment drive. A settled colour change can take many periods. Raising the SPI transfer speed would affect image upload time, which falls outside these BUSY measurements. A faster panel PLL mode above 120 Hz has yet to be verified on frame-2.

For **white → red**, a shorter 4.032 s sequence produced 1 dark and 0 bright flashes. Its target distance was 0.176. The selected clip shows the closer red. The comparison CSV includes both recipes.

On frame-2 we prepared six rows with a native colour refresh before each trial. Seven columns then received different partial-drive recipes simultaneously. The same layout appeared on both panel controllers. A separate column supplied a control. Across 7 trials we tested **49 recipes per starting colour**, including individual selectors, ordered stages and repeated pulses. The programme durations ranged from **0.405 to 6.046 seconds**. The measured drive includes any trailing neutral periods. In the recipe readout, “S” identifies a source selector, the number after × gives the number of periods for that selector, and parentheses group repeated stages.

For each target we compared the final camera RGB of both patches with all six native colours measured in the same column before the partial drive. Each row was normalised to its nearby starting white. **Target distance** is the root-mean-square channel difference in these camera-relative units. Smaller values are closer. Among matches we selected the shortest drive, breaking ties by flash count and then colour distance. If no recipe matched, we preferred candidates that placed the target first on **both** controllers. Among those, or across all recipes if none did, we kept candidates within 0.005 of the lowest worst-controller distance and applied the same tie-breaks. The displayed crop, timing curve and flash counts come from the upper controller. Both halves contribute to the colour assessment.

A **match** reaches the intended nearest colour on both halves with a worst distance of at most 0.10. An **approximation** reaches that nearest colour but exceeds the tolerance. A **miss** means at least one half ended closest to a different reference. Several requests can therefore select the same physical recipe or patch. Each run started from a freshly prepared native colour. Repeated partial updates can start with a different pigment distribution.

## What happens inside the ink

Spectra 6 uses four pigments to produce six visible panel colours. An electric field moves charged pigment particles through the ink. Their distribution near the viewing surface changes which wavelengths are scattered back or absorbed. Green and black are possible without dedicated green or black particles. Because the starting distribution differs, applying the same field sequence to two starting colours can give different endpoints. In the illustration, the strip at the top of each cell is a measured colour. The particle positions underneath are a schematic explanation. The camera measures reflected light at the surface. We used the **1600 × 1200 OpenPaper L** on frame-2 with a DKE DEPG1330RTE133F5HP panel. A native refresh first set the six starting rows.
