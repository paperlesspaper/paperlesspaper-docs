<Callout type="info" title="Rendering photos on OpenPaper 7">

Use the six colors measured on the panel when calculating Floyd–Steinberg dithering. On one 7.3-inch OpenPaper 7, this reduced mean photo color error from **5.42 to 3.33 ΔE₀₀** and made dark brown and teal patches smoother. Both versions took one ordinary full refresh.

</Callout>

## Measured colors in the ditherer

The center column of each group models black, white, red, yellow, blue and green by their ideal RGB values. The right column models the same six pixel codes using their measured appearance on this panel. Look at the astronaut’s face and the change in color between the coffee and the rim of the cup. Both versions were on screen together. The second capture swapped their physical positions.

## Choosing colors for each image

With the stock firmware, each pixel has six standard color choices. For a photo full of oranges, the renderer can vary the proportion of red and yellow pixels to match the fruit. A mixture still occupies several pixels, and the pattern can be visible up close. The OpenPaper 7 charts include measured mixtures with 25%, 50% and 75% red, each paired with yellow. [Measured mixture swatches](/panels/dke-7-inch#mixtures).

For a flat illustration, choosing the closest measured mixture for each large color area could give a more consistent orange. For a photograph, reducing the source to six image-specific colors first can discard shading before dithering starts. A more promising experiment would keep the original gradients and tune the red/yellow mixture where orange matters, while preserving small accents elsewhere. The photo trials above tested a fixed measured palette. Whether image-specific mixtures improve on it remains open.

A uniform orange pixel would require a repeatable drive recipe. If a larger set of solid tones becomes available in one refresh mode, the renderer could select orange for one image and a skin tone for another. Extra solid tones and direct 16-code rendering still await verification on the 7.3-inch panel.
