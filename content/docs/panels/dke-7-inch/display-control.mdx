<Callout type="info" title="Rendering photos on OpenPaper 7">

Use the six colors measured on the panel when calculating Floyd–Steinberg dithering. On one 7.3-inch OpenPaper 7, this reduced mean photo color error from **5.42 to 3.33 ΔE₀₀** and made dark brown and teal patches smoother. Both versions took one ordinary full refresh.

</Callout>

## More contrast and richer color

**The moderate boost gave the best balance for this photo.** On frame-1, the orange areas gained 21% in chroma and 25% in lightness contrast, with similar average color error. All three versions used the same six measured panel colors and Floyd–Steinberg dithering. Only the contrast and chroma of the target image changed before dithering.

Drag the divider or use the arrow keys to compare the photographs. Each slider shows the same crop photographed at the same physical position on the panel in separate refreshes.

Look at the pale rim of the cut fruit and the shadow beneath the whole orange. The peel gains color while the flesh and rind remain distinct.

**Which colors made the orange?** In the moderate version, the orange parts of the fruit used **71.29% red, 23.47% yellow and 5.20% white** pixels, plus 13 green pixels (0.04%). The white share in the fruit fell from 11.3% in the reference. The white background accounts for most of the white pixels in the complete photo. [Pixel counts, settings and measurements](/panels/dke-7-inch#orange-photo-contrast-and-chroma).

The center column of each group models black, white, red, yellow, blue and green by their ideal RGB values. ## Choosing colors for each image

With the stock firmware, each pixel has six standard color choices. For a photo full of oranges, the renderer can vary the proportion of red and yellow pixels to match the fruit. A mixture still occupies several pixels, and the pattern can be visible up close. The OpenPaper 7 charts include measured mixtures with 25%, 50% and 75% red, each paired with yellow. [Measured mixture swatches](/panels/dke-7-inch#mixtures).

We also tested red/yellow mixtures tuned to the oranges in this photograph. Allowing neutral pixels made the orange more accurate but increased grain. Restricting orange areas to red and yellow made uniform patches smoother and slightly increased photo color error. [Photographs and results of the mixture trials](/panels/dke-7-inch#redyellow-mixtures-in-the-orange-photo).

A uniform orange pixel would require a repeatable drive recipe. If a larger set of solid tones becomes available in one refresh mode, the renderer could select orange for one image and a skin tone for another. Extra solid tones and direct 16-code rendering still await verification on the 7.3-inch panel.
